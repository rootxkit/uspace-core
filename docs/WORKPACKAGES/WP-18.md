## What to build

```go
// Destination solves the direct geodesic problem on WGS84.
func Destination(from core.LatLon, bearingDeg, distanceM float64) core.LatLon
```

Vincenty's direct formulae, so that it is the exact counterpart of the
`Inverse` already in core: same ellipsoid constants, the same series for
`A` and `B`, iterated on sigma to `1e-12` rad (bounded by
`maxIterations`; the iteration is a contraction by about `f` and settles
in a handful of steps for any distance).

- `bearingDeg` is degrees clockwise from true north; any finite value is
  taken modulo 360.
- `distanceM` is a geodesic length in metres, `>= 0`; zero returns `from`
  unchanged.
- The result's longitude is wrapped into `[-180, 180]`, so a destination
  across the antimeridian is a valid position.
- From a pole the bearing is measured from the meridian of
  `from.LonDeg`, as `Inverse` measures it.
- An invalid `from` (non-finite or out of range), a non-finite bearing,
  or a negative or non-finite distance returns
  `core.LatLon{LatDeg: NaN, LonDeg: NaN}`, which is not `Valid()`. No
  error value: like `HaversineM` and `LocalOffsetM` it is a computation,
  not a decoder, and the caller checks `Valid()`. It never panics.

What it must not do: change `Inverse`, `DistanceM`, any other function,
constant or counter. No new counter: nothing is refused or dropped
inside the library.

## The promise

For a valid `from`, a finite bearing and `0 <= distanceM` well short of
the antipode (where `Inverse` itself does not converge), `Inverse(from,
Destination(from, bearing, d))` returns `d` and the bearing to under
0.1 mm on the ground, and `Destination(a, Inverse(a, b))` lands within
0.1 mm of `b`.

## Tests

- Geoscience Australia's example: Flinders Peak, 306 52 05.37,
  54 972.271 m lands on Buninyong within the rounding of the published
  bearing (0.005 arc-second, 1.33 mm sideways at that range); the round
  trips below pin the sub-millimetre agreement.
- Round trip both ways with `Inverse` over seeded random pairs up to
  10 000 km: distance and bearing within 0.1 mm.
- Antimeridian, both directions: the longitude wraps and the round trip
  holds.
- Near the poles (89.9999 and -89.9999) and from a pole exactly: valid
  results, the round trip holds; due north across the pole comes back
  down the opposite meridian.
- Zero distance returns `from` exactly; each refusal (invalid `from`,
  NaN or infinite bearing, negative, NaN or infinite distance) beside its
  accepted twin (E-01).
- `FuzzDestination` over every input: never panics; an invalid input
  gives NaN; a valid one gives a valid position whose distance from
  `from` by `Inverse` is the input within 1 mm, up to 1000 km.
- `BenchmarkVincentyDirect` with a target in `docs/bench-targets.txt`
  (2000 ns, as the inverse).
